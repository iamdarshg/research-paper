\section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}
The empirical evidence in this revision is intentionally narrow. It consists of public-geometry smoke training on VSP Airshow models, a higher-resolution Airshow addendum, bounded smoke runs on synthetic or procedural data retained for context, a reduced freeform-object sweep for the historical CFD path, and smoke-level checks for the new structured-conditioning seam. These artifacts are reported as code-path validation and implementation evidence rather than as a definitive aircraft-design benchmark.

\subsection{Public Airshow Corpus Training Smoke Evidence}
To replace the earlier synthetic-only scale-up evidence, we built a public VSP Airshow corpus from model documents exposed by the Airshow web app. OpenVSP describes itself as a parametric aircraft geometry tool, and the OpenVSP project site links VSP Airshow as a public model exchange \cite{openvspGithub,openvspLicense,vspAirshow}. The corpus builder admits only CC0, CC BY, and CC BY-SA Airshow entries with public preview geometry; it excludes NC and ND entries from the training manifest. In the run reported here, the builder observed 381 public model documents, found 357 license-and-geometry-eligible documents, and converted 355 records into centered \(16^3\) voxel grids. The two rejected eligible documents had stale public storage URLs returning 404.

\subsection{Airshow Resolution Sweep and Loss Debugging}
We also reran the Airshow corpus path at \(32^3\), \(64^3\), and \(96^3\) to check whether higher voxel count alone would clear the generated-aircraft validity gate. All three higher-resolution manifests passed the claim-bearing manifest validator with the same 355 converted public Airshow records as the \(16^3\) run. The \(32^3\) training run completed one epoch with batch size 2 and produced checkpoint hash \texttt{7234e1b9b338...b95444}. The \(64^3\) corpus was also manifest-valid, but the attempted batch-size-1 training run did not produce a checkpoint before the local 15-minute run ceiling. This is an implementation limit as well as an empirical result: the dense voxel decoder maps 2048 hidden units to \(grid\_resolution^3\) outputs, so the final decoder layer grows from roughly 67.1 million parameters at \(32^3\) to 537.1 million at \(64^3\) and 1.812 billion at \(96^3\).

The \(32^3\) manifest hash was \texttt{7684e70b...98521f1}, the \(64^3\) manifest hash was \texttt{2627227f...4a1dbd80}, and the \(96^3\) manifest hash was \texttt{86f2a112...118e7cd}. The \(32^3\) generated cases are shown in Figures \ref{fig:airshow_flight_metrics_g32} and \ref{fig:airshow_generated_geometry_g32}. Their occupancy fractions were 0.00494, 0.00476, and 0.00510. Their raw internal D3Q27 \(C_d\) values were finite, but all three failed the aircraft-validity screen: two failed \texttt{nonempty\_occupancy}, \texttt{symmetry}, and \texttt{span\_sanity}, while the third failed \texttt{symmetry} and \texttt{span\_sanity}. The result is therefore negative evidence against treating resolution alone as enough to make the present checkpoint claim-bearing.

We then filtered the \(32^3\) corpus with the same aircraft-validity screen used for generated outputs. This kept 176 of 355 public Airshow records and produced a claim-bearing-valid manifest hash \texttt{0d149d98...d25ccc}. An initial fine-tune from the one-epoch \(32^3\) checkpoint exposed a zero-learning-rate resume issue: the run completed, but checkpoint comparison showed byte-identical model weights. After restoring the configured learning rate on resume, a three-epoch source-valid fine-tune produced checkpoint hash \texttt{657243ea...45574c}. Its three generated cases all passed occupancy and symmetry but still failed \texttt{span\_sanity}, with length-fraction values 0.25, 0.25, and 0.28125. A further three-epoch continuation regressed validity, with two cases failing both \texttt{symmetry} and \texttt{span\_sanity}. This suggests that the present objective and decoder need architectural or optimization changes, not simply more epochs.

The \(96^3\) follow-up replaced the dense decoder with a coordinate decoder that evaluates sampled \((z,y,x)\) coordinates conditioned on the latent vector. The public \(96^3\) source corpus had mean occupancy 0.00565 and median occupancy 0.00458, so naive coordinate sampling either collapsed toward empty fixed-threshold grids or, when occupied coordinates were oversampled without correction, biased the decoder toward overly dense outputs. The final run used importance-weighted coordinate BCE for three epochs over all 355 records and produced checkpoint hash \texttt{1bfdcfcf...59774d3}. With the default 0.5 threshold, all three flight-path exports remained below the aircraft-validity occupancy gate. With a post-hoc top-k export calibrated to 0.75\% occupancy \emph{against the same heuristic screen used as the outcome}, one of three cases, \texttt{endurance\_turning}, passed; \texttt{short\_takeoff\_payload} and \texttt{high\_speed\_sprint} still failed \texttt{symmetry}, \texttt{span\_sanity}, and \texttt{wing\_body\_balance}. This is not an uncalibrated generation-rate result: it shows that the threshold can be fitted to the screen at \(N=3\), not that the model reliably generates valid aircraft. Figures \ref{fig:airshow_flight_metrics_g96_topk} and \ref{fig:airshow_generated_geometry_g96_topk} show that calibrated export result. This is evidence that the coordinate-decoder path can train and export \(96^3\) geometry, not evidence of reliable aircraft generation or validated aerodynamic optimization.

The same debugging pass clarified the zero aerodynamic and connectivity observations. The exact connectivity score thresholds voxel probabilities and runs connected-component labeling through NumPy/SciPy. The raw aerodynamic score thresholds geometry, invokes the internal solver, and returns scalar solver outputs. A local gradient probe confirmed that these measured scores do not provide analytic PyTorch gradients by themselves. The training code was therefore revised to report \texttt{optimization\_loss} separately from detached monitor terms and to add a direct solver-in-loop loss that calls the internal D3Q27 LBM evaluator during training and estimates its backward signal with two-sided SPSA finite differences. This is a measured black-box gradient estimator around real solver calls, not a learned substitute for the solver.

The first direct-solver sweep was intentionally diagnostic. A naive 0.5 probability threshold produced empty or effectively empty scheduled geometries, so the direct solver objective stayed nearly constant. A 5\% top-k occupancy target then made the solver-visible shapes too dense relative to the public Airshow corpus, whose mean occupancy was 0.01495 at \(32^3\), 0.00802 at \(64^3\), and 0.00565 at \(96^3\). The final sweep therefore used a 1\% top-k target, a low-frequency \(8^3\) perturbation field upsampled to the solver grid, a direct-solver loss weight of 0.2, three D3Q27 solver steps per scheduled objective call, and zero detached aerodynamic/connectivity monitor intervals. That setting spends the full-grid compute on the loss being optimized rather than on diagnostics that cannot change the weights.

\subsection{Generator Recovery: Root Causes and Held-Out Generation Validity}
\label{sec:generator_recovery}

The preceding subsections report that the Airshow-checkpoint outputs fail the aircraft-specific screen at every tested resolution. This revision treats that failure as a defect to be diagnosed and repaired, and reports a separate CRM recovery under a declared protocol.

\paragraph{Measured root causes.}
Five causes were isolated on the recording host, each by direct measurement rather than
inference.

\emph{(i) The training corpus was largely invalid under our own screen.} The procedural
corpus generator carried a private gate (occupancy $\ge 0.0005$, span $\ge 0.03$,
symmetry $\ge 0.10$) that is far laxer than \texttt{CLI/aircraft\_validity.py}. Re-running
the pre-fix generator over the eight families (seed 42), its own gate accepted 47 of 48
draws, of which \textbf{1} passes the declared screen. A model trained on that corpus was
therefore asked to reproduce shapes the paper itself calls invalid.

\emph{(ii) The shipped checkpoint's decoder emits a near-constant field.} On the only
checkpoint in the tree ($16^3$), four seeds give probability min/max $0.435$--$0.573$ and
a materialised occupancy of $45.2$--$46.4\%$: a solid block with
\texttt{geometry\_probability\_std} $\approx 0.0075$. A constant field crossing the $0.5$
threshold fills the lattice, so \texttt{span\_sanity}, \texttt{normalization\_margin}
and \texttt{grounded\_occupancy\_envelope} fail by construction.

\emph{(iii) The decoder was trained ten times slower than the rest of the model.} The
\texttt{train} CLI exposes \texttt{--learning-rate}, but the optimizer's three parameter
groups take their rates from \texttt{CLI/config.yaml}, where
\texttt{converter\_learning\_rate} and \texttt{consistency\_student\_learning\_rate} are
both $2\times10^{-5}$; \texttt{--learning-rate} sets only the diffusion group. Holding
everything else fixed on the $32^3$ corpus, the decoder reconstruction loss is flat at
the legacy rate ($1.672\to1.663$ over 12 epochs, probability standard deviation
$0.007$) and falls at $2\times10^{-4}$ ($1.666\to1.396$, standard deviation $0.188$). The
only module that can turn a latent into occupied voxels was effectively frozen.

\emph{(iv) Generation depended on an under-trained consistency student.} The shipped
four-step path samples with \texttt{student\_model}, whose RMS drifted to $21.8$--$179$
against a teacher RMS of $0.66$--$3.3$ during the recovery runs
(\texttt{consistency\_raw\_mse} $465$--$32212$). The denoiser trained by the denoising
MSE (loss $0.86$--$3.4$) was never used for sampling.

\emph{(v) Two corpus-scale limits.} The structured latent pools a $180$-value multi-scale
geometry signature into \texttt{latent\_dim} values, and the training split contains six
records. At the shipped $\text{latent\_dim}=16$ the reconstruction of a held-out $32^3$
record reaches IoU $0.390$ but is inflated $2.4\times$ in occupancy, failing only the
screen's upper normalization bound.

\paragraph{Repairs.}
The procedural generator now uses the declared screen as its own gate, enforces exact
bilateral symmetry about the screen's mid-plane, gives every aircraft an explicit
longitudinal centre body with blunt ends, and resamples samples into a declared
fill/occupancy band inside the screen's normalization margins. The \texttt{train} CLI
gains \texttt{--converter-learning-rate} and
\texttt{--consistency-student-learning-rate} with legacy defaults, and the generator
gains a deterministic teacher-DDIM sampler (\texttt{sampler="ddim"}) alongside the
shipped four-step consistency path. No threshold inside \texttt{aircraft\_validity.py}
was changed.

\paragraph{Held-out protocol.}
The evaluation protocol is recorded in
\texttt{build/recovery\_2026-11-05/PREREGISTERED\_PROTOCOL.md}. Its file timestamp does
not establish ordering relative to the evaluation runs, so this paper calls the protocol
\emph{declared} rather than pre-registered. Training uses the six
NASA CRM records labelled \texttt{test}/\texttt{val}; the nine \texttt{holdout} records
are never used for gradients, threshold selection, early stopping or checkpoint
selection. The materialisation threshold is a fixed, uncalibrated $0.5$; the screen is
the unmodified \texttt{evaluate\_aircraft\_validity} with canonicalization on. Three arms
are reported separately: ground truth (the screen's ceiling on this split),
reconstruction (decoder given the record's own latent, i.e.\ decoder capacity only), and
generation (sampling from noise conditioned on the record's condition vector, nine
records $\times$ five seeds).

The recovery checkpoint ($32^3$ dense decoder, latent 16, 45 epochs, corrected
parameter-group learning rates) passes the declared protocol: reconstruction
9/9 and generated 45/45 under both samplers, with a failed-check histogram that is
empty, and the same 45 artifacts re-screen as 45/45 through the shipped
\texttt{CLI/aircraft\_validity.py}. Independent ablations show that the decisive
change was decoder supervision density, not simply more epochs: the dense decoder
emits a logit for every one of the 32\,768 voxels per step, whereas the coordinate
decoder supervises 8\,192 sampled voxels with a small Fourier MLP and plateaued with a
diffuse probability field (0/9 reconstruction at $\text{latent\_dim}=16$ and $64$).

The recovery is not conditioned or diverse generation, and the paper does not report it
as such. The 45 generated artifacts contain \emph{five} distinct voxel arrays (maximum
pairwise Hamming distance one voxel, mean pairwise IoU 0.99905); within a condition the
five seeds are not bit-identical, yielding 1--4 distinct arrays. The nine held-out
records are also only six distinct voxel arrays, because four aeroelastic variants are
sub-voxel duplicates at \(32^3\). The held-out latent codes are nearly degenerate
(pairwise distance 0.0027 against a latent norm of 0.497), the training split holds six
records, and the MSE objective's optimum is therefore the conditional mean.

An adversarial audit of this result found two facts that bound the claim more sharply
than the pass rate suggests. First, every occupied voxel emitted by the checkpoint is a
voxel present in the training corpus (precision $1.000$ against the training union), so
there are zero novel voxels. Second, a model-free constant array, the voxel-wise union
of the six training geometries, passes the same screen 12/12 and covers 98.6--99.4\% of
every held-out record. The screen also accepts a non-aircraft equal-arm plus sign and a
three-box primitive. The 45/45 rate therefore demonstrates that a training-hull-like
emitter satisfies the current heuristic gate; it does not demonstrate that the checkpoint
generates new aircraft. A pass-rate metric cannot distinguish this from a generator,
which is itself the paper's central measurement finding. A non-trivial gate must add a
novelty and diversity requirement (distance from the training set, distinct-geometry
count, and condition-response spread) and must include a negative control set that
non-aircraft primitives fail.
\paragraph{Status of the generation gate.}
Gate 1 of Table~\ref{tab:claim_gates} (\emph{generates aircraft structures}) is met in
its literal form: multiple generated samples from a held-out split pass the
aircraft-specific checks at an uncalibrated threshold, and the artifacts re-screen
through the repository's own CLI. The gate is \emph{not} met in its scientific form:
the emitted voxels are all present in the training corpus, a model-free constant passes
the same screen, and non-aircraft primitives pass as well. The
remaining work is therefore corpus scale and conditioning, not geometric validity: the
repository now has a working path from a trained checkpoint to aircraft-valid voxel
geometry at $32^3$ on an 8\,GiB host, and it has a measured demonstration that validity
alone does not imply generative diversity. Aerodynamic, structural and manufacturing
claims remain blocked, as before.

\subsection{Freeform-object Sweep: Steps vs. Shape Prior}
We ran a compact sweep over generation step count and the shape-prior cleanup threshold. The sweep evaluated 12 settings: consistency steps in \{1,2,4,8\} crossed with minimum connected-component sizes in \{0,32,64\}. For each setting we generated one freeform object, applied the post-processing hook, and computed aerodynamic metrics using the CPU CFD path. The sweep provides only a preliminary probe of how post-processing thresholds can alter the generated object before scoring; the preceding textual protocol record documents the coverage.

The reduced sweep covered four consistency-step settings crossed with three cleanup thresholds. Because each setting used one sample and the CPU CFD path is not externally validated, this coverage is retained as a textual protocol record rather than a results figure.

The occupancy-versus-score question is left as a protocol hypothesis rather than plotted with authored coordinates. No measured relationship is claimed from the one-sample-per-setting sweep.

\subsection{Freeform-object Geometry Summary}
The generated freeform objects are still dense and near-threshold before cleanup, but the new plausibility prior reduces isolated components and slightly lowers the occupancy fraction. For this implementation path, that matters more than small changes in the raw voxel logits: the cleaned binary shapes are less fragmented and more suitable for downstream meshing and export. This should not be read as evidence that the outputs satisfy aircraft-specific geometry checks or manufacturing constraints.

\subsection{Hyperparameter and Tuning Notes}
The sweep was intentionally small but targeted for the current codebase: it varied the inference path length and the new connected-component cleanup threshold. The training-side change was also incremental: a voxel-shape prior was added to the loss to discourage midpoint noise and disconnected fragments. A larger future study should sweep latent dimension, shape-prior weights, and higher CFD resolutions.

\subsection{Structured-Conditioning Smoke Evidence}
This revision also adds a documented 22-slot condition schema together with dataset, model, generator, and offline-densification plumbing that consumes the resulting condition vector. We include a smoke-level condition-response summary on the procedural path to check whether different condition payloads produce non-identical changes in simple geometry proxies such as occupancy, span, and engine-related heuristics. That evidence is intentionally weak: it shows that the condition path is wired and that the procedural prior is not condition-invariant, but it does not validate mission-conditioned or manufacturing-conditioned aircraft generation on grounded aircraft-like data.

\subsection{Publication-Readiness Correction: Consistent Reference-Area Convention}
\label{sec:reference_area_correction}
An internal audit (2026-11-01) found a genuine normalization defect in the frozen low-Mach comparison corpus. The OpenFOAM side normalized $C_d$ and $C_l$ using a \emph{fixed} sweep reference area (\(A_{\mathrm{ref}} = 0.27734375\)), while the internal D3Q27 solver normalizes by its own voxel-projected frontal area (the $y$--$z$ projection of the solid mask), which is resolution-dependent: \(0.2850196\) at grid 32 vs.\ \(0.2385490\) at grid 64 for the frozen biplane corpus, i.e.\ a \(\pm 16\%\) swing on the same geometry. Dividing the two coefficient sets directly (as the original comparison scripts did) therefore contaminated \texttt{cd\_error\_percent} by the normalization mismatch, and the single previously reported ``0.25\%'' passing row (Mach 0.3, OpenFOAM grid 64, LBM grid 64) was a coincidence of normalizations rather than physical agreement.

The comparison convention is now fixed and enforced by regression tests (\texttt{tests/test\_reference\_area\_regression.py}): \emph{both} solvers' coefficients are expressed on the \emph{same} reference area before any error is computed. Concretely, the raw LBM coefficient is renormalized onto the OpenFOAM sweep area,
\[
C_{\mathrm{LBM,corr}} = C_{\mathrm{LBM,raw}} \times \frac{A_{\mathrm{ref,LBM}}}{A_{\mathrm{ref,sweep}}},
\]
which turns \texttt{cd\_error\_percent} into a pure force comparison on one documented convention. Both reference areas are recorded per row (\texttt{openfoam\_reference\_area}, \texttt{lbm\_reference\_area}) together with the convention label \texttt{lbm\_renormalized\_to\_openfoam\_reference\_area}. The recomputation script is \texttt{scripts/recompute\_corpus\_consistent\_reference\_area.py} and the corrected tables are written under \texttt{evidence/research-paper-publish/corrected\_corpus/}.

Table \ref{tab:frozen_corpus_corrected} gives the corrected agreement of the frozen corpus under the repository's own gate (Cd error \(< 5\%\), \texttt{MINIMUM\_ACCEPTABLE\_ERROR\_PERCENT} in \texttt{run\_internal\_benchmark.py}). OpenFOAM is not installed in this environment, so the frozen OpenFOAM rows are kept verbatim as documented frozen artifacts; only the comparison convention changed.

\subsection{Statistical Substance of the Condition-Response Sweep}
\label{sec:condition_response_stats}
The prior smoke checkpoint's condition-response evidence was limited to \(n=4\) seeds, where target speed versus measured drag showed \(r=-0.806\) with \(p=0.19\): a direction-only, non-significant observation. To obtain a real effect estimate with uncertainty intervals, we ran the canonical \texttt{validate-conditions} step at \(n=12\) seeds (grid 32, 100 solver steps) against the same smoke checkpoint (\texttt{checkpoints\_protocol\_smoke/final\_optimized\_model.pt}); artifact \texttt{build/protocol\_smoke/condition\_validation\_n12.json}, analysis script \texttt{scripts/condition\_response\_stats\_n12.py}.

Two important measurement facts came out of that run that bound how the numbers may be read:

1. \textbf{Solver instability events.} Two of twelve seeds produced non-physical D3Q27 outputs (drag magnitudes \(\approx 10^{22}\) and \(\approx 10^{33}\), seeds 1 and 10 respectively) for otherwise ordinary conditions (speed 38.1 m/s / span 2.22 m and speed 64.3 m/s / span 1.71 m). These are checkpoint-level solver blow-ups at grid 32 / 100 steps on the smoke checkpoint, not physical drag values. Any claim that ignores or silently removes these seeds would be cherry-picking; the paper instead reports both the full set and the set excluding the two documentable instability events.
2. \textbf{Run-to-run variation on identical seeds.} The same four seed conditions that produced the October \(n=4\) artifact (measured\_drag mean \(1.977 \pm 0.302\)) produced different values in this run (seeds 0--3: 1.396, 5.819e33, 1.771, 2.269) on the same checkpoint and the same CUDA hardware. The smoke checkpoint's D3Q27 measurement is therefore not bit-reproducible run-to-run; point coefficients must be treated as estimates with spread, not as constants.

With \(n=12\) (Fisher-z approximation, two-sided \(\alpha=0.05\)), the design is powered to detect \(|\rho| \approx 0.78\) at \(\sim80\%\) power; smaller effects are not resolvable at this sample size and are reported as non-significant with wide confidence intervals. Table \ref{tab:condition_stats} gives both the raw \(n=12\) correlations and the \(n=10\) set excluding the two blow-up seeds.

\subsection{Regime Limits and Honest Scope of the CFD Evidence}
\label{sec:regime_limits}
The internal D3Q27 solver is a weakly compressible, isothermal low-Mach sanity path. Its documented validity boundary is Mach \(\leq 0.3\) with claim grade \texttt{low\_mach\_sanity\_only} and compressibility model \texttt{weakly\_compressible\_isothermal\_lbm}. The paper makes no high-Mach, transonic, or supersonic validation claim, and no claim that the internal solver reproduces compressible-flow physics; Mach numbers above 0.3 remain experimental and unvalidated externally (frozen audit boundary, reconfirmed 2026-11-01). The frozen low-Mach corpus itself does not meet the repository's own \(<5\%\) Cd agreement gate (Table \ref{tab:frozen_corpus_corrected}), so this paper does not claim validated low-Mach agreement with OpenFOAM either; it reports the measured disagreement as the honest current status.

Similarly, the mixed-precision document's expected 2.2\(\times\) speedup for the RTX 4060 is a planning estimate, not a measured result in this revision; no speedup claim from that table is carried into this paper. The FP16/FP32 path's density error (\texttt{MIXED\_PRECISION\_GUIDE.md}) is a solver-level quantity and is not claimed as a pipeline-level accuracy result. The aircraft-validity screen also stays bounded: frozen NASA CRM whole-aircraft voxels pass 15/15, while the bundled root STLs (biplane, F-18, cylinder, 20mm cube) fail 0/4, with quantified failure modes such as \texttt{normalization\_margin}, \texttt{span\_sanity}, and \texttt{tail\_body\_plausibility} (harness: \texttt{scripts/agent\_d\_validity\_harness.py}). The screen is a first-pass shape-plausibility heuristic, not structural, aerodynamic, or manufacturing validation.

\subsection{OpenFOAM Export Path and Tiered Ground Truth}
The OpenFOAM export path now writes a plain \texttt{forces} function-object dictionary, and the resulting force vector is normalized manually to recover coefficients when coefficient output is not available. The current benchmark still shows a substantial Cd gap, which we treat as a solver- and sampling-consistency diagnostic rather than as resolved by paper-level normalization alone. A higher-confidence CFD comparison will still need a more systematic hyperparameter sweep and consistent normalization of reference area and flow conditions; the reference-area convention in Section \ref{sec:reference_area_correction} is now that consistent normalization, and the remaining gap is physical/statistical rather than definitional.

The current ground-truth foundation is therefore tiered. Internal D3Q27 LBM values can drive the direct SPSA training loss and sequential candidate selection, but they stay in the internal-label tier. The OpenFOAM/simpleFoam and sonicFoam scripts define the external PDE route: cases must complete, residual and field-export checks must pass, and the resulting labels must remain separate from internal LBM labels until they satisfy the \texttt{external\_pde} gate. This separation is important for future PDE validation because it lets the repository compare internal LBM coefficients against OpenFOAM under shared geometry, Mach, Reynolds, grid, and reference-area settings without silently upgrading the internal solver to ground truth.

We also recorded one physics correction in \texttt{CLI/cascaded\_lbm.py}. The D3Q27 freestream setup now uses the lattice-consistent value \(u = Ma / \sqrt{3}\); the earlier version used arbitrary scaling. That correction is part of the benchmark history, and we are checking it against the Cd mismatch before making broader coordinate-transform changes.

\subsection{The Single Decisive Final Run}
\label{sec:final_run}
The checked-in final protocol (\texttt{CLI/run\_protocols/gcp\_128\_295m.yaml}, executed by the monitored runner) is a future experiment for the remaining scale, diversity, conditioning, and solver-loop questions; it is not the first evidence that voxel geometry can pass the aircraft-validity screen. Section~\ref{sec:generator_recovery} reports a completed \(32^3\) CRM recovery on this host, in which 45/45 generated samples pass the uncalibrated screen on held-out records, but the same geometry is emitted across conditions and seeds. The \(128^3\) protocol trains the scaled architecture (512-dim latent, coordinate decoder width 2048 depth 5, 70.1M trainable parameters) in bfloat16 for two epochs at batch size 1 on the pinned-latent training corpus, checkpointing every 25 updates under the interruption-safe runner, with the fused-stream BFL solver backend and a GPU-side exact-SDF attestation. Because the run is monitored, it produces the run-state lineage (checkpoint hashes, manifest hashes, protocol hashes) needed by the evidence package rather than an unrepeatable training history.

The remaining upgrade questions for this run are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Generation diversity:} do the generated samples contain distinct aircraft-like geometries, rather than the single geometry measured in the \(32^3\) recovery?
    \item \textbf{Condition-response signal:} does a grounded condition benchmark show directional signal on held-out records rather than condition-invariant generation?
    \item \textbf{Solver-in-loop signal:} does the scheduled direct D3Q27 SPSA objective decrease over the run instead of drifting, with the evidence package recording it?
    \item \textbf{Lineage-consistent evidence:} does \texttt{CLI/final\_evidence.py} return \texttt{pass} with matching run/checkpoint/manifest/protocol hashes?
\end{enumerate}

The literal validity-screen gate is passed on the declared CRM held-out split at \(32^3\); the unresolved scientific gate is diverse, meaningfully conditioned aircraft generation. The \(128^3\) run tests that broader claim at higher resolution but does not replace the need for larger, diverse training data. Even a fully passing run does not establish structural load-path viability, superiority over mature baselines, or externally validated aerodynamic optimality, which require the separate structural, baseline, and PDE-validation studies listed in Sections \ref{sec:baselines} and \ref{sec:validation}.
