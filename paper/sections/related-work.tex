\subsection{Diffusion and Generative Modeling Foundations}
Foundational research has established diffusion-based models as a stable alternative to GANs. Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) \cite{ho2020denoising} and their improved variants \cite{nichol2021improved} provide high-fidelity sampling. Score-based models \cite{song2021score} and their continuous-time SDE formulations \cite{song2021score} offer a rigorous mathematical framework for generation, while recent work has further elucidated the broader design space and sampler choices for these diffusion models \cite{karras2022elucidating}. Consistency Models \cite{song2023consistency} further improve training and inference speed by distilling these paths into fewer steps. Our work builds on these foundations, utilizing a 128D latent space to manage the computational cost of 3D grids, which we evaluate through multi-step and 1-step ablations.

\subsection{3D Shape Generation}
3D generative models cover voxels \cite{wu20163d}, point clouds \cite{achlioptas2018point, luo2021diffusion}, meshes \cite{wang2018pixel2mesh}, and implicit surfaces \cite{mescheder2019occupancy}. While these models achieve strong results on visual datasets like ShapeNet \cite{chang2015shapenet} and PartNet \cite{mo2019partnet}, they often prioritize visual mimicry over the physical manifold integrity required for engineering. Our framework addresses this by integrating semantic part labeling and a structural connectivity prior, which we intend to validate against traditional voxel-GAN baselines.

\subsection{Generative Design and Topology Optimization}
Topology Optimization (TO), specifically the SIMP method \cite{sigmund2001topology}, is the industry standard for load-path optimization \cite{sigmund2013topology}. Recent neural TO approaches \cite{sosnovik2019neural} accelerate this process. However, TO is often sensitive to local minima and initial conditions. Our diffusion-based approach identifies aerodynamic forms that might be unreachable by gradient-based material redistribution alone. We plan to compare our results against SIMP-optimized forms to evaluate structural material efficiency.

\subsection{Aerospace and Aerodynamic Shape Optimization}
Modern aerodynamic shape optimization (ASO) relies on adjoint methods for gradient calculation \cite{jameson1988aerodynamic, jameson1995optimum} and surrogate modeling for efficient design space exploration \cite{forrester2008engineering}. Our framework uses an integrated D3Q27 LBM solver to provide simulation-in-the-loop 3D aerodynamic feedback, positioning it as an exploratory tool that complements local adjoint-based optimization. The preliminary accuracy of this feedback is documented in Section \ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Physics-Informed and Simulation-in-the-Loop ML}
Physics-informed machine learning incorporates PDEs through PINNs \cite{raissi2019pinn} or Neural Operators like FNO \cite{li2020fourier}. Our work follows a simulation-in-the-loop paradigm where a GPU-accelerated LBM solver provides guidance during training. This enables multi-fidelity label promotion (from LBM to OpenFOAM), which we intend to use for generating ground truth fields for our PINN-ready benchmarks.

\subsection{Constraint-Aware Generation}
Enforcing physical validity—such as manufacturability and connectivity—remains a core challenge. Oh et al. \cite{oh2019deep} demonstrated generative design for structures, while Steinmetz et al. \cite{steinmetz2022generative} utilized differentiable projection for manufacturing. Our implementation extends this through a persistent Constraint Projector and semantic repair adapter designed to ensure designs comply with aircraft-specific clearance and connectivity rules. The effectiveness of these constraints is partially probed by our cleanup-threshold sweep in Section \ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Novelty and Positioning}
This work is distinguished from prior art as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{vs. 3D Diffusion:} We prioritize physical manifold integrity and connectivity through a specialized semantic geometry system and BFL-consistent momentum exchange calculation.
    \item \textbf{vs. Topology Optimization:} We leverage the exploratory power of diffusion to identify aerodynamic forms outside the convergence basin of gradient-based material redistribution.
    \item \textbf{vs. CFD Surrogates:} Our framework uses simulation-in-the-loop feedback rather than a static surrogate, allowing for dynamic adaptation to 3D boundary conditions.
\end{itemize}
This "Thinking in Diffusion and Speaking in Autoregression" (TiDAR) approach allows for Design Space exploration while documenting preliminary validation checks in Section \ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Experimental Baseline Selection}
\label{sec:baselines}
To rigorously evaluate the proposed framework, we define a set of baseline comparisons and ablation studies. These baselines are selected to provide a fair context for evaluating generative expressiveness, aerodynamic efficiency, and structural viability.

\subsubsection{Baseline Families}
We compare our method against three primary families of baselines:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Classical Parametric Design:} We utilize a library of parametric aircraft primitives, specifically swept and tapered wings based on NACA 0012 and 4412 airfoil profiles. Parameters include aspect ratio (4--12), sweep angle (0--45$^\circ$), and taper ratio (0.4--1.0). This baseline represents traditional engineering "priors" and tests whether the generative model can discover forms with comparable or superior efficiency.
    \item \textbf{Unconstrained Generative 3D:} We implement a voxel-GAN \cite{wu20163d}, a point-cloud diffusion model \cite{luo2021diffusion}, and an implicit occupancy network \cite{mescheder2019occupancy} trained on the same synthetic aircraft dataset but without the aerodynamic loss term or connectivity priors. These were selected as representative baselines for their respective 3D representations (voxel, point cloud, and implicit) to isolate the contribution of our specific guidance mechanisms.
    \item \textbf{Optimization-based Search:} We compare against two search strategies: (a) a SIMP-based topology optimization \cite{sigmund2001topology} for load-path efficiency, and (b) a surrogate-based search where 1,000 random design candidates are sampled and ranked by the same AeroSurrogate model used in our framework.
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Ablation Studies}
We investigate the contribution of individual components through the following internal ablations:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Physics Ablation:} Training without the $\mathcal{L}_{aero}$ term to evaluate the specific impact of CFD guidance on the resulting $L/D$ distribution.
    \item \textbf{Connectivity Ablation:} Training without the $\mathcal{L}_{conn}$ term to measure the "natural" structural connectivity of the diffusion process.
    \item \textbf{Cleanup Ablation:} Disabling the connected-component shape-prior cleanup hook to isolate the effect of post-processing on manifold integrity.
    \item \textbf{Projection Ablation:} Disabling the semantic `ConstraintProjector` to evaluate the effectiveness of the repair-based projection versus the raw model output.
    \item \textbf{Consistency Sweep:} Evaluating inference performance and quality across consistency step counts $N \in \{1, 2, 4, 8\}$.
\end{itemize}
