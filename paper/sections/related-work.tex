\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

The convergence of generative modeling and computational engineering has transformed the landscape of structural and aerodynamic design. This section contextualizes our framework within the broader literature of 3D generative models, topology optimization, and physics-informed machine learning.

\subsection{3D Generative Modeling and Diffusion}
Early research into 3D shape generation relied heavily on Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) and Variational Autoencoders (VAEs). While pioneering, these methods frequently faced challenges in maintaining structural manifold integrity and generating high-resolution voxels. The emergence of Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) \cite{ho2020denoising, nichol2021improved} provided a more stable training objective and superior sample diversity. Recent advancements have extended diffusion to grid-based 3D representations \cite{zhou20213d} and functional spaces like Occupancy Networks \cite{mescheder2019occupancy}. Unlike general-purpose 3D diffusion models trained on datasets like ShapeNet \cite{chang2015shapenet}, our approach prioritizes structural connectivity and physics-based viability over pure visual mimicry.

\subsection{Generative Design and Topology Optimization}
Generative design has traditionally been synonymous with Topology Optimization (TO), particularly the Solid Isotropic Material with Penalization (SIMP) method \cite{sigmund2001topology, bendsoe2013topology}. TO iteratively redistributes material to minimize compliance under structural loads. While mathematically rigorous, TO is often computationally expensive and sensitive to initial conditions. Modern deep learning approaches attempt to accelerate this process by learning to predict optimized topologies directly. Our work differs from classical TO by adopting a "Sample-Rank-Validate" loop, leveraging the exploratory power of diffusion models to discover novel aerodynamic forms that might lie outside the convergence basin of gradient-based TO.

\subsection{Aerodynamic Optimization and CFD-in-the-Loop}
The use of machine learning for aerodynamic shape optimization has historically focused on airfoil sections \cite{chen2019deep}. Integrating high-fidelity Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) into the training loop is often prohibited by the iterative nature of Navier-Stokes solvers. To circumvent this, researchers have employed surrogate models or Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINNs) \cite{raissi2019pinn} to approximate flow fields \cite{thuerey2020physics}. Our framework advances this by utilizing a GPU-accelerated D3Q27 Lattice Boltzmann Method (LBM) solver. This allows for simulation-in-the-loop training where aerodynamic coefficients $C_L$ and $C_D$ provide direct guidance to the generative process, rather than relying on a static pre-trained surrogate.

\subsection{Constraint-Aware Generation}
Enforcing hard physical constraints—such as structural connectivity and manufacturing feasibility—remains a core challenge in generative design. Hu and Zhang \cite{hu2023physics} demonstrated the use of physics-constrained loss functions to guide generative models. Our implementation utilizes a specialized Connectivity Loss and a semantic geometry system to ensure that generated aircraft structures are not only aerodynamically efficient but also structurally coherent and manufacturable.

\subsection{Novelty and Positioning}
The primary novelty of this work lies in the synthesis of TRM/HRM principles with a high-fidelity cascaded LBM solver within a diffusion-based pipeline. While prior works have explored 3D diffusion or CFD surrogates in isolation, our framework is the first to achieve a tightly coupled loop where a 128D latent space is mapped to a 3D physical domain, validated against OpenFOAM-calibrated LBM physics in real-time. This "Thinking in Diffusion and Speaking in Autoregression" (TiDAR) approach allows for the parallel exploration of the design space while maintaining the rigorous validation standards required for aerospace engineering. Our use of a D3Q27 Cascaded MRT solver provides a stability-to-accuracy ratio superior to the D3Q19 models commonly found in real-time solvers, as evidenced by our canonical benchmark results in Section \ref{sec:results}.
