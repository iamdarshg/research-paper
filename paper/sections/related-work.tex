\subsection{Diffusion and Generative Modeling Foundations}
Foundational research has established diffusion-based models as a stable alternative to GANs and VAEs. Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) \cite{ho2020denoising} and Score-Based models \cite{song2021score} provide high-fidelity sampling but typically do not enforce hard structural constraints. Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) \cite{rombach2022latent} improve efficiency by operating in compressed spaces. Our work utilizes a 128D latent space and a progressive refinement schedule to manage the computational cost of 3D grids, which we evaluate in our training convergence studies.

\subsection{3D Shape Generation}
3D generative models cover voxels \cite{wu20163d}, point clouds, and implicit surfaces \cite{mescheder2019occupancy}. While these models achieve strong results on visual datasets like ShapeNet \cite{chang2015shapenet}, they often produce fragmented "voxel clouds" that are unsuitable for engineering analysis. Our framework addresses this limitation by integrating a structural connectivity prior and semantic part labeling, which we validate against traditional 3D-GAN baselines.

\subsection{Generative Design and Topology Optimization}
Topology Optimization (TO), specifically the SIMP method \cite{sigmund2001topology}, provides a mathematical basis for material distribution. Recent neural TO approaches \cite{sosnovik2019neural} accelerate this process. However, TO is often sensitive to local minima and initial conditions. Our diffusion-based approach identifies aerodynamic forms that might be unreachable by gradient-based redistribution alone. We use classical SIMP as a baseline for structural material efficiency.

\subsection{Aerospace and Aerodynamic Shape Optimization}
Aerodynamic design has evolved from classical Hicks-Henneke parametrizations to adjoint-based methods \cite{jameson1988aerodynamic} and deep learning surrogates \cite{chen2019deep}. Adjoint methods are efficient for local refinement but struggle with topological changes. Our framework uses a D3Q27 LBM solver to provide real-time 3D aerodynamic feedback, positioning it as an exploratory tool that complements local adjoint-based optimization. The accuracy of this feedback is verified against OpenFOAM in Section \ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Physics-Informed and Simulation-in-the-Loop ML}
Physics-informed machine learning incorporates PDEs through PINNs \cite{raissi2019pinn} or Neural Operators \cite{li2020fourier}. Our work follows a simulation-in-the-loop paradigm where a GPU-accelerated LBM solver provides active guidance during training. This enables multi-fidelity label promotion (from LBM to OpenFOAM), providing ground truth data for the PINN-ready fields exported by our pipeline.

\subsection{Constraint-Aware Generation}
Enforcing physical validity—such as manufacturability and connectivity—remains a core challenge. Oh et al. \cite{oh2019deep} demonstrated physics-constrained design, while Steinmetz et al. \cite{steinmetz2022generative} utilized differentiable projection for manufacturing. Our implementation extends this through a persistent Constraint Report system and a semantic adapter that ensures every design complies with symmetry and structural connectivity rules. The effectiveness of these constraints is evaluated in our connectivity penalty sweep.

\subsection{Novelty and Positioning}
This work is distinguished from prior art as follows:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{vs. 3D Diffusion:} We prioritize physical manifold integrity over visual synthesis through a specialized BFL-consistent momentum exchange loss.
    \item \textbf{vs. Topology Optimization:} We leverage diffusion to identify novel aerodynamic forms outside the convergence basin of material redistribution.
    \item \textbf{vs. CFD Surrogates:} Our framework uses simulation-in-the-loop feedback rather than a static surrogate, allowing for real-time adaptation to boundary conditions.
\end{itemize}
This "Thinking in Diffusion and Speaking in Autoregression" (TiDAR) approach allows for Design Space exploration while maintaining the validation standards documented in Section \ref{sec:results}.
