This section contextualizes our framework within the broader literature of diffusion models, 3D generation, topology optimization, and physics-informed machine learning.

\subsection{Diffusion and Generative Modeling Foundations}
Foundational research has shifted from GANs toward diffusion-based models. Denoising Diffusion Probabilistic Models (DDPM) \cite{ho2020denoising} and Score-Based models \cite{song2021score} provide stable objectives for high-fidelity generation. Latent Diffusion Models (LDM) \cite{rombach2022latent} further improve efficiency by operating in compressed spaces. Our work builds on these principles, utilizing a 128D latent space and a progressive grid refinement schedule (16--32\textsuperscript{3}), which we evaluate in our training convergence studies.

\subsection{3D Shape Generation}
Generative models for 3D data cover voxels \cite{wu20163d}, point clouds, and implicit surfaces \cite{park2019deepsdf, mescheder2019occupancy}. While these models achieve impressive visual results on datasets like ShapeNet \cite{chang2015shapenet}, they often produce "voxel clouds" that lack the structural integrity required for engineering. Our framework addresses this by integrating semantic part labeling and a structural connectivity prior, validated against traditional 3D-GAN baselines.

\subsection{Generative Design and Topology Optimization}
Topology Optimization (TO), particularly the SIMP method \cite{sigmund2001topology}, is the industry standard for material distribution. Recent neural TO approaches \cite{sosnovik2019neural} learn to map boundary conditions to optimized structures. Unlike TO, which is sensitive to initial seeds and local minima, our diffusion-based approach adopts a "Sample-Rank-Validate" loop to explore a wider design space. We compare our results against classical SIMP to evaluate structural efficiency.

\subsection{Aerospace and Aerodynamic Shape Optimization}
Aerodynamic optimization has evolved from classical Hicks-Henneke parametrizations to adjoint-based methods \cite{jameson1988aerodynamic} and deep learning surrogates \cite{chen2019deep, thuerey2020physics}. While adjoint methods are efficient for local refinement, they struggle with global topology changes. Our framework uses a D3Q27 LBM solver to provide real-time 3D aerodynamic feedback, positioning it as an exploratory tool that complements local adjoint-based refinement.

\subsection{Physics-Informed and Simulation-in-the-Loop ML}
Physics-informed machine learning incorporates PDEs through PINNs \cite{raissi2019pinn} or Neural Operators like FNO \cite{li2020fourier}. Our work follows the simulation-in-the-loop paradigm, where a GPU-accelerated LBM solver provides real-time guidance. This allows for multi-fidelity label promotion (from LBM to OpenFOAM), providing high-fidelity ground truth for the PINN-ready fields exported by our pipeline.

\subsection{Constraint-Aware Generation}
Enforcing physical constraints is critical for engineering. Oh et al. \cite{oh2019deep} and Hu et al. \cite{hu2023physics} demonstrated physics-constrained design using specialized loss functions. Our implementation extends this through a persistent Constraint Report system and a semantic geometry adapter that ensures every generated aircraft complies with symmetry, clearance, and structural connectivity rules.

\subsection{Novelty and Positioning}
The novelty of this work lies in the following distinctions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{vs. 3D Diffusion:} Unlike models focused on visual synthesis, we prioritize physical manifold integrity and connectivity through a specialized BFL-consistent momentum exchange loss.
    \item \textbf{vs. Topology Optimization:} We leverage the stochastic exploration of diffusion to identify aerodynamic forms that lie outside the convergence basin of gradient-based material redistribution.
    \item \textbf{vs. CFD Surrogates:} Our framework uses real-time simulation-in-the-loop feedback rather than a static surrogate, allowing the model to adapt to complex 3D boundary conditions dynamically.
\end{itemize}
This "Thinking in Diffusion and Speaking in Autoregression" (TiDAR) approach allows for the parallel exploration of the design space while maintaining the rigorous validation standards required for aerospace engineering.
